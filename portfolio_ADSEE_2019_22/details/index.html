Project in depth

Applied computer vision, analytics and data-science education

Developed a four-stage computer-vision case for ADSEE at Algebra / Algebra LAB, turning street video into detected objects, cropped faces and results that learners could explore in tables and charts. The work combined applied software development with Jupyter notebooks, practical exercises and teaching materials that explain both what the models can do and where their results need careful interpretation.

Client / context
Erasmus ADSEE · Algebra / Algebra LAB
Period

A practical retail and marketing case

The case used street video to explore how visual data can support questions about activity in a location. Learners followed the process of preparing images, detecting objects and examining the resulting records.

My contribution joined the working examples with their explanation: preparing the material, developing the analysis workflows, presenting the results and making each stage usable in a guided teaching session.

Video preparation and sample creation

Prepared a workflow to load source video, extract images at a selected interval and save the resulting sample for later processing. The teaching example sampled one frame every five seconds, making a longer street scene manageable for step-by-step experimentation.

The notebook and accompanying presentation explain the input parameters and intermediate outputs. Learners can inspect the extracted images and try a different short video before moving on to detection.

Object detection and structured results

Applied existing computer-vision models to the prepared images to detect objects such as people, bicycles, cars and bags. The workflow produces detection images and tabular results, retaining the position in the source video so a result can be traced back to the scene.

Results can be exported to Excel and inspected alongside the corresponding images. This connects the model output with familiar analytical tools and makes it easier to check what was actually detected.

  • Object class and detection probability for examining model output.
  • Video timestamps for selecting and comparing parts of the recording.
  • Images associated with detection records for visual review.
  • Excel output for further inspection outside the notebook.

Interactive filtering and visual analysis

Built result views that combine tables with images and allow filtering by time in the video, object class and detection probability. A practical exercise combines these controls to select person detections from a chosen time interval above a selected probability threshold.

The material also demonstrates interactive charts, including a horizontal bar chart grouped by object class. These views help learners move from individual detections to a structured comparison of the results, while retaining access to the underlying images.

Face extraction and detector tuning

Developed the next stage around locating faces in the street images and saving face crops for subsequent experiments. The teaching sequence compares initial results with revised detector settings before applying the chosen configuration to the image set.

The example filters out small face crops using a minimum image-size condition. This provides a concrete way to explain why distant faces, resolution and detection settings affect what later models receive.

Experimental facial-attribute analysis

Used DeepFace-based analysis to explore estimates such as age and gender from the extracted face images. This stage builds on existing models and frameworks; the project contribution is their integration into the case, the preparation of suitable inputs and the analysis and explanation of the outputs.

The exercises compare results from street-video crops with results from clearer images. They explicitly discuss imperfect predictions and the effect of input quality, treating the outputs as experiments rather than reliable facts about a person.

Four connected notebooks and practical teaching materials

Organised the case into four linked parts: data selection and sample preparation; object detection and result analysis; face detection and crop preparation; and experimental attribute analysis. Each part has a corresponding Jupyter notebook and presentation.

The teaching materials walk through the relevant cells, input parameters, generated files and visual results. Exercises ask learners to run individual steps, inspect intermediate outputs, adjust filters and discuss the model's limitations.

Set up a local Jupyter server on Algebra infrastructure for the prepared material and student demonstrations. This brought the software and its explanation into the same practical learning environment.

Educational structure and platform design

Contributed to the design of the educational modules and proposed a platform arrangement that combines an LMS for course content with Jupyter-based interactive examples. The structure separates reading and presentation material from live code, visualisation and hands-on exercises.

The module framework connects the business question with data selection, choice of methods, model development, analysis of results and discussion of operational use. This provides a reusable teaching structure around the implemented retail case.

Project context and published educational output

ADSEE was an Erasmus+ collaboration between five partners: Algebra, the University of Amsterdam, TIB, the Faculty of Information Studies in Novo mesto and Arctur. It aimed to make applied data science accessible to higher-education learners and business users.

The project published its educational materials in November 2021, identifying Algebra as the authoring institution for the Retail/Marketing module. The case described here formed part of that educational contribution; the wider consortium developed the other use cases and programme activities.
