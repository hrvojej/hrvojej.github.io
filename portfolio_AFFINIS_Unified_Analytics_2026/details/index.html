AFFINIS unified analytics for eADR and eFTI

Authored the technical design for extending the existing AFFINIS big-data lakehouse with eADR and eFTI/NSCP data. The work joined source-system analysis, shared dimensional models, processing and reporting specifications, analytical validation and an infrastructure-upgrade assessment.

Scope and delivered work

This was a distinct analytics design assignment associated with the eADR engagement, building on the existing AFFINIS road-transport platform. Its purpose was to bring the eADR and eFTI/NSCP datasets and their reference codes into a common analytical environment alongside existing Taho/SOTAH data.

I prepared three connected packages: the foundational analytical project design, focused requirements for validating the analytical processes and reporting, and an assessment of the infrastructure expansion. Delivery to the AKD project team is documented in February 2026, followed by revisions to the wider validation specification in March.

The foundational design was delivered as a working technical baseline for the analytical-validation scope and further implementation planning. The contribution described here is the architecture and documentation delivered to the client.

Understanding the source domains

Started with the business meaning of the source records: dangerous-goods registers, inspections, incidents, training and permits in eADR; freight consignments, transport operations and electronic freight information in eFTI/NSCP; and the existing tachograph domain.

Specified which datasets and reference codes enter the analytical platform, the owner and meaning of each source, transfer cadence and basic completeness checks. The design separates operational records from the derived analytical views used for supervision and reporting.

Existing tachograph analytics provide context for the new cross-domain analysis. The new assignment adds and relates eADR and eFTI data rather than presenting the earlier AFFINIS implementation as a new deliverable.

Ingestion and reference-data alignment

Documented PostgreSQL change capture for eADR, with a file-transfer fallback, and scheduled file-based ingestion for NSCP/eFTI. The specification covers structured CSV/JSON extracts and the interpretation and validation of eFTI XML payloads.

Defined format checks, source identifiers, duplicate handling, error paths and change handling. Reference-code mappings align different source representations before they become shared analytical attributes.

The integration design retains the information needed to determine what arrived, from which source and at what time, so that later reconciliation can distinguish a missing source record from a transformation error.

Raw, Silver and Gold responsibilities

Designed the processing boundaries across a Medallion-style lakehouse. Raw preserves incoming source records in MinIO; Silver holds cleaned and harmonised columnar data; Gold publishes the analytical tables used by reporting.

Specified Spark/PySpark processing and Parquet outputs, followed by loading into StarRocks. The documentation addresses partitioning, load ordering, repeatable loading and quality checks before publishing analytical results.

This separation makes it possible to trace and reprocess the affected stage without obscuring the original input. The design connects storage and processing decisions to the business need for explainable analytical figures.

Shared identities and dimensional models

Defined shared vehicle, driver and carrier keys so that records from different domains can refer to the same real-world entity. The design includes source-code harmonisation, entity matching, history handling and rules for resolving conflicting attributes.

Prepared dimensional and fact-table structures, bridge relationships and analytical views. Different grains remain explicit: a consignment, an inspection, a measure, an incident and a training event represent different business facts.

The technical documentation describes table relationships, slowly changing dimensions, example SQL and how shared identities support cross-domain analysis. Personal identifiers and access restrictions are handled within the specified privacy and governance boundaries.

Orchestration, quality and source-to-report traceability

Specified Dagster workflows for source processing, reference-code synchronisation, cross-domain outputs and quality reporting. Dependencies make the order of ingestion, transformation, publication and downstream reporting explicit.

Defined checks for completeness, duplicate records, valid references, schema consistency and business-rule compliance. Failed checks lead to an identified handling path and evidence rather than an unexplained change in a dashboard total.

The lineage design links a KPI and its filters to the Gold records, processing run, intermediate objects and original source extract or change event. This supports both technical diagnosis and explanation of the reported metric to business users.

KPIs and Apache Superset reporting

Turned business questions into definitions of analytical measures and report datasets. The scope includes inspection and incident analysis, freight activity, cross-domain subject profiles, time trends and data-quality monitoring.

Specified dimensions, filters, metric interpretation, report access and drill-down paths. Composite risk indicators are documented analytical rules; the contribution does not depend on an unverified machine-learning model or an assumed improvement in operational outcomes.

Mapped the reporting layer to StarRocks analytical structures and Apache Superset, including the relationship between user roles, row-level restrictions and the data visible in a report.

Infrastructure upgrade and operational requirements

Prepared the technical basis for expanding the existing lakehouse to support the combined workloads. The resource assessment covers expected ingestion, processing and retention, then identifies the required storage, compute, analytical, network and operational components.

Defined minimum technical requirements suitable for procurement and the checks needed after installation: configuration, load, security and functional verification. The operational scope includes monitoring, alerting, backups, recovery and maintenance responsibilities.

The architecture documentation also addresses deployment and orchestration on Kubernetes, service dependencies and continuity planning. Capacity figures are planning inputs, so the portfolio does not present them as measured production throughput.

Analytical validation and acceptance evidence

Prepared a validation scope that is distinct from testing the operational eADR application. It follows the analytical chain: complete source ingestion, correct cleaning and matching, accurate KPI/report calculation, access control and source-to-report reconciliation.

Defined the need for representative samples, expected results, pass criteria, defect records and corrective actions. These provide a basis for checking an eventual implementation against the agreed meaning of the data.

Updated the wider verification and validation documentation in response to AKD comments, including the definition and consistency of specialist responsibilities and qualification requirements. This connects technical scope with the service specification used for delivery and procurement.

Relationship to the wider portfolio

The eADR project describes solution architecture, business and system analysis, API integration, technical coordination and UAT. This separate case explains the additional Data Architect contribution to the unified analytics expansion.

The earlier 2024 road-transport analytics case covers the original AFFINIS big-data architecture and implementation documentation. The 2026 work described here expands that baseline with the new eADR and eFTI/NSCP analytical domains.
