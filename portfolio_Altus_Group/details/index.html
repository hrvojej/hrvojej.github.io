Project in depth

Unified marketing data models in Snowflake and dbt

Developed dbt and Snowflake models that connected marketing activity with Salesforce campaigns, leads, opportunities and bookings for Altus Group. The work brought paid advertising, email and web-analytics information into a shared analytical environment and made complex campaign hierarchies usable for reporting. A major focus was understanding what each measure represented and how to combine it without losing the relationship between campaigns and sales outcomes.

Connecting marketing spend with the sales funnel

The business needed to follow marketing activity beyond clicks and impressions: which campaigns generated leads, which leads became qualified or accepted by sales, and which progressed to opportunities and bookings. Campaigns also belonged to hierarchies of different depths, with costs and engagement measures recorded at different levels.

My role combined data modelling, SQL development, source investigation and report validation. I developed the campaign and funnel models within an existing dbt/Snowflake environment and worked through the differences between CRM, advertising and analytics data.

Bringing channels into a common analytical structure

Worked with Salesforce, Google Ads, Bing Ads, LinkedIn Ads, Google Analytics, Pardot and email-related data in Snowflake, using Matillion for ingestion and data preparation and dbt for transformations and analytical models.

Standardised the paid-media sources into common campaign, advertisement, date, device, currency and performance fields. Preserved the source and marketing medium so that search advertising and paid social could be compared while retaining their differences. Where a platform did not supply a field, the model represented that absence explicitly.

  • Common measures included spend, clicks, impressions, conversions and social engagement.
  • Campaign naming conventions and lookup tables supplied business unit, region, country, language, audience or keyword theme, targeting and match type.
  • Campaign and external platform identifiers connected advertising records to the corresponding Salesforce campaigns.

Reconstructing Salesforce campaign hierarchies

Developed recursive SQL models that followed campaign-to-parent relationships and reconstructed hierarchies of one to four levels. Exposed the hierarchy as consistent columns so that reporting could show the overall initiative, its intermediate campaigns and the final campaign associated with activity.

Built separate models for the different hierarchy depths and combined them into common outputs. Added campaign status, dates, external platform identifiers and financial and engagement measures from the relevant campaign record.

Handling measures recorded at different hierarchy levels

A parent campaign can appear repeatedly when joined to several children. Summing its cost or email counts on every row would overstate the result. Used window functions and conditional projections to carry repeated parent measures once in flattened outputs.

Also developed a common child-level representation for hierarchies of different depths. Shorter hierarchies were aligned to the same final campaign position. Additional logic compared parent email and cost measures with child totals and represented activity recorded at the parent level in the child-level output.

Prepared a separate hierarchy-cost view that presented each campaign level as rows, supporting inspection of costs at the overall initiative, intermediate campaign and child campaign levels.

Campaign members, email engagement and lead qualification

Connected campaign hierarchy data with campaign-member records and lead data. Calculated sent and responded member counts, distinguished leads from contacts, and included email deliveries, unique opens and tracked-link clicks.

Carried lead qualification and conversion fields through the models, including marketing-qualified leads, sales-accepted leads and converted leads. Preserved the dates, source, reason and campaign references needed to investigate how leads entered and progressed through the funnel.

  • Linked leads to the lowest existing campaign rather than assuming every campaign had the same number of hierarchy levels.
  • Retained Pardot and UTM attributes, including first-touch and last-touch fields, for source and attribution investigation.
  • Kept campaign-level aggregates and individual lead records distinct when deciding how results should be counted.

From leads to opportunities and bookings

Joined campaign-linked leads to opportunity and booking information through the converted-opportunity reference. Brought together opportunity status, created and won counts, closed and lost counts, and monetary measures needed to relate marketing activity to sales outcomes.

Prepared daily aggregates based on lead creation dates, retaining the campaign hierarchy and qualification flags. Worked with existing booking and currency-converted financial measures so that funnel reporting could use consistent definitions already present in the warehouse.

Daily performance and reporting models

Developed campaign/day advertising aggregates for spend, impressions, clicks, conversions and likes, including CPM calculated from total spend and impressions. Combined the advertising side with campaign, lead and booking models to prepare reporting views across the funnel.

Created reporting variants for the full hierarchy and for campaigns aligned to the child level. These included email engagement, campaign-member counts, qualified leads and created or won opportunities alongside paid-media measures. Extracted additional reporting dimensions such as year, business unit, region, offer, campaign theme and tactic from campaign naming.

Google Analytics and attribution investigation

Investigated Google Analytics 4 campaign and acquisition information alongside advertising and Salesforce records. Used campaign identifiers, source and medium, Google Ads references, conversion events and website engagement measures to examine how activity could be related across systems.

A central issue was the meaning of the dates and attribution fields. A lead can arrive before recorded ad spend or after advertising ends, and a shared web form can receive both paid and organic traffic. Investigated these mismatches and documented where source attribution needed clarification before campaign performance could be interpreted reliably.

Validation with Tableau, Power BI and source comparisons

Prepared Tableau and Power BI views to inspect campaign structures, costs, raw hierarchy measures, email fields and campaign-to-lead relationships. Used these views to compare the warehouse outputs with Salesforce and to make differences visible during discussions with stakeholders.

Performed focused checks on campaign identifiers, missing advertising references, member and lead counts, qualification filters and the risk of counting the same activity more than once. Compared the new daily reporting outputs with existing qualified-lead reports and documented discrepancies and follow-up questions.

Model organisation, handover and practical value

Organised the work across staging, transformation, analytical marts and reporting models, with dbt references making dependencies explicit. Documented the campaign hierarchy logic, the purpose of successive funnel models and the meaning of the final reporting outputs.

The result was a connected analytical foundation for examining marketing activity from campaign structure and channel engagement through to qualified leads and sales opportunities. It also gave the team a clearer way to discuss data quality, attribution assumptions and which measures could be compared or aggregated.
