Project in depth

Survey-data integration, warehousing and Power BI

Developed the Python/SQL data preparation and warehouse foundation for Hendal’s survey and fieldwork analytics, together with Power BI dashboards and DAX calculations. The project brought project-specific survey tables from four MySQL databases into a common analytical structure.

The source-system problem

Each research project had its own table structure. The source estate contained approximately ten million records and grew by about one million records per year, making repeated manual consolidation unsuitable for continuing reporting.

Worked through extraction and restructuring tasks that had remained unresolved in an earlier external engagement. Selected relevant attributes from the source tables and prepared a shared structure for cross-project analysis.

Extraction, harmonisation and warehouse preparation

Used Python and SQL to extract and clean the survey records from four MySQL databases. The processing copied selected attributes into common tables and supported nightly enrichment and harmonisation.

This provided the data foundation for fieldwork analysis across projects and interviewers. The analytical subsystem was designed for analysis and visualisation of telephone-survey data, rather than survey-data entry.

Power BI and DAX development

Developed dashboards and visualisations in Power BI with DAX calculations over the prepared data. The work connected survey timing, contact outcomes, quotas and interviewer activity with the reporting requirements.

The specification requested up to fourteen interactive reports or combinations of views based on fourteen existing reporting needs. The following table describes that requested analytical scope; it is not a claim of separately documented final acceptance of every view.

Fourteen fieldwork reporting requirements

The reporting brief reached beyond headline counts to the operational causes of interviewer and dialler performance.

AreaRequired view
1 · Survey timingWhen interviews were conducted
2 · DurationTotal interview duration
3 · Section timingDuration of individual questionnaire sections where timestamps exist
4 · GapsTime between successful interviews
5 · ThroughputInterview counts by interviewer, region and overall
6 · Contact outcomesContacts and refusals, distinguishing answered and unanswered calls
7 · Dialler activityBackground calling and interviewer waiting time
8 · QuotasSuccessful interviews against required quotas
9 · Response reviewInterviewer comparison through answers to key questions
10 · EfficiencyTime, contacts and successful-interview proportions by person, region and project
11 · Quality controlRecording review and supervisor ratings, including ratings without a recording
12 · PaymentInterviewer calculations using adjusted or manually entered rates
13 · Active interviewersParticipation-based activity with an explicitly defined time window
14 · Briefing participationRecording attendance in project instructions/briefings

Dependencies that affected the report design

Some questions depend on information being captured correctly upstream. Section-duration analysis requires questionnaire timestamps; payment analysis requires the appropriate historical rates; an active-interviewer view requires agreement on the time window that defines activity.

These dependencies make the reporting specification more precise. They prevent a visualisation from implying that missing or undefined source information has already been resolved.

Delivery context and knowledge transfer

The specified environment used MySQL and Power BI on the client’s infrastructure, with collaboration from people familiar with the source system. Testing and confirmation of the results were part of the delivery framework.

The project conditions also included explaining the capabilities and limitations of the tools and infrastructure. The implemented contribution covered the extraction/restructuring solution, shared analytical foundation and Power BI/DAX work.
