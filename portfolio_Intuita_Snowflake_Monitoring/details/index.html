Snowflake cost and performance monitoring design

Researched and specified an internal Snowflake cost and performance monitoring solution for Intuita Consulting. Connected credit consumption, warehouse activity, query behaviour and storage policies with the information teams need to manage spending and choose optimisation work.

Purpose and scope

Investigated how an internal Snowflake monitoring solution could explain resource consumption and help teams decide where to optimise. The scope connected costs with warehouse activity, query performance, storage policies and responsibility for individual workloads.

The work focused on research and solution definition: identifying useful measurements, comparing available monitoring approaches and setting out the requirements for an interactive dashboard or application.

Cost categories and monitoring data

Separated virtual-warehouse compute, serverless processing, cloud services, storage and data transfer so each cost category could be examined using the appropriate usage information.

Reviewed Account Usage and Information Schema as sources for historical and operational reporting. The proposed views included credit consumption by warehouse, user and time period, alongside monthly storage and service-level breakdowns.

Warehouse and query performance

Examined warehouse sizing, suspension and resumption, concurrency and query acceleration as decisions that affect both response time and credit consumption.

Reviewed query history and operator-level statistics for investigating expensive or slow SQL. The analysis covered scanned data, partition pruning, local and remote spilling, join behaviour, execution-time breakdowns and the volume of rows produced by individual operators.

Storage, retention and data movement

Documented the cost implications of active tables, staged files, retained historical data and clones. Compared permanent, transient and temporary tables in terms of recovery requirements and the cost of keeping data that can be recreated.

Included cross-region and cross-cloud movement, replication, unloading and external-function traffic in the cost model. These were separate considerations from query execution and warehouse consumption.

Budgets, accountability and operational response

Reviewed budgets and resource monitors as complementary ways to observe consumption, notify responsible teams and control warehouse usage.

Considered how workload or department reference data and object tags could support showback and internal cost allocation. The proposed operating approach included meaningful alert conditions, named ownership and a response process for investigating unexpected consumption.

Dashboard and application options

Reviewed native Snowflake views, BI dashboards and specialist monitoring products. Considered an interactive Streamlit application where teams could maintain the reference data needed to group workloads and explain costs.

The intended next steps were a design presentation, a demonstration and a repeatable deployment approach. The analysis established the functional scope and evaluation criteria for that development.

A monitoring model tied to engineering decisions

The research connected each proposed measurement with a decision that the team could take. This avoids a dashboard that reports consumption without explaining what to investigate.

AreaInformation to examineDecision supported
Warehouse computeCredit use, activity, concurrency and workload timingSizing, suspension/resumption and workload placement
Query executionOperator rows, scanned partitions, pruning, elapsed time and spillingWhich SQL or data-layout problem to investigate
Serverless servicesConsumption attributed to background servicesWhether service configuration and usage justify the cost
StorageActive data, historical retention, clones and staged filesRetention, recovery and lifecycle policies
Data movementCross-region/cloud transfer, replication and external-function trafficWhere and how data should be processed or shared
AccountabilityDepartment/workload reference data and object tagsShowback, cross charging and named cost ownership

Access model and application requirements

The internal brief called for custom roles and appropriate grants rather than routine use of top-level administrative roles. The proposed monitoring application therefore needed access to usage data without making every consumer an account administrator.

Considered Streamlit for an interactive layer in which users could maintain workload or department mappings. Compared that approach with native Snowsight views, existing BI tools and specialist products.

The intended delivery sequence was a comparison of alternatives, a design presentation, a dashboard or application, a demonstration and a repeatable deployment process. The completed contribution documented here is the research and solution definition; those requested implementation outputs are not asserted as delivered.
