Project in depth

An open lakehouse for national road-transport analytics

Designed an open-source lakehouse and analytical approach for national road-transport data. Specified how tachograph records, inspection data and ministry information would be combined, together with the infrastructure, governance and procurement requirements for delivery.

Client / context
Agency for Commercial Activities (AKD) / Croatian Ministry of the Sea, Transport and Infrastructure
Period
July – December 2024 · subsequent occasional advisory
My role
Data Architect / Technical Lead

In this detail

  1. Turning transport questions into analytical requirements
  2. Governance and personal-data handling
  3. Analytical subjects and the questions they support
  4. Platform responsibilities in the proposed architecture
  5. From architecture to procurement and team capability
← Project overview

Turning transport questions into analytical requirements

Defined more than 40 indicators covering safety, compliance and operating efficiency. Examples included drivers’ rest periods, speed patterns and vehicle use.

Specified analysis by transport company, individual driver, groups of drivers and fleet, with operational and regulatory filters. Considered different generations of tachograph cards and the need to connect their records with inspections.

Governance and personal-data handling

Specified requirements for data access, ownership, quality and privacy. Addressed the responsibilities needed to operate the platform and to use driver-related data for defined analytical purposes.

Connected governance with the processing design instead of treating it as a separate document to be added after implementation.

Analytical subjects and the questions they support

The design joins tachograph information with inspections and ministry data so that analysis can move between a driver, a vehicle, a transport company and a fleet. Supporting different card generations and delivery structures is part of preparing those records for common analysis.

Analytical areaQuestions represented in the requirements
SafetySpeed patterns and conditions requiring investigation
ComplianceDriver rest periods and links with inspection evidence
Operational efficiencyVehicle use and comparison across fleets or groups of drivers
Search and explorationCompany, individual driver, driver group and fleet filters

Platform responsibilities in the proposed architecture

Designed for a planned scope exceeding 250 TB, more than 75,000 drivers and 65,000 vehicles.

The architecture assigned separate roles to storage, processing, transformations, analytical serving and governance. This helped connect a technology choice with the capability that the delivery team actually needed.

ResponsibilityProposed components
Object storageMinIO
Distributed processingApache Spark
Transformation and orchestrationdbt and Dagster
Analytical serving and explorationStarRocks and Apache Superset
Metadata and governanceOpenMetadata
Supporting relational servicesPostgreSQL
Platform operationKubernetes and associated infrastructure automation

From architecture to procurement and team capability

Prepared requirements for both test and production infrastructure, processing behaviour and data protection. The project context was approximately €2 million, with hardware procurement of approximately €250,000; these are project/procurement figures rather than personal delivery value.

Connected RFI/RFP specifications and the EU-funded procurement process with technical discussions involving the selected partner. Assessed the capabilities needed in DevOps, data engineering and analytics so that the chosen architecture had identifiable implementation and operational owners.

The planned 250 TB+, 75,000+ drivers and 65,000+ vehicles are architecture-sizing inputs. The work described is platform and analytics design, specification and technical leadership, with subsequent advisory involvement.
