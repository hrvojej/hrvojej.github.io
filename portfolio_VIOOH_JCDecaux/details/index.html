Project in depth

SQL and dbt engineering for real-time advertising data

Developed and maintained Snowflake/dbt models for VIOOH / JCDecaux’s programmatic advertising data. The work included nested-JSON transformations, supply/demand reconciliation, pacing calculations, historical backfills, late-arrival analysis and recommendations for real-time reporting.

Advertising events and the processing environment

The platform handled approximately 1–5 million complex JSON records per hour. Advertising applications fed Kafka, a sink persisted data in S3, and Airflow coordinated loading into Snowflake for downstream transformation and analytics.

Worked across the source events, loading structures, staging models and analytical outputs. The business questions concerned advertising inventory, allocations, bids, sales, impressions, revenue and campaign pacing.

Three related transformation streams

Developed and revised SQL that separates supply, successful demand and unsuccessful demand. Each stream has a different event structure and grain before it is brought into a shared reporting model.

StreamTransformation work
SupplyFlatten deal metadata, retain market/deal/device/time context and aggregate offered quantities and impressions
Demand salesFlatten sales arrays; aggregate quantities, plays, impressions, clearing values and spend
Demand no-salesFlatten unsuccessful-sale arrays; retain loss reasons and calculate unsold quantities and impressions

Nested JSON, event identity and reporting grain

Used Snowflake VARIANT access and FLATTEN to extract nested arrays and structures. Selected and cast the required attributes before grouping the records for reporting.

Investigated duplicate bundle identifiers and the fields needed to join sales and no-sales data. Revised analysis includes bundle identity and creation time where those fields are needed to distinguish events.

The final reporting joins use the relevant market, deal, account/DSP, placement and trade-hour dimensions. Correct grain matters because flattening multiple arrays or joining aggregates at inconsistent levels can multiply quantities.

Metric calculations and unit handling

Worked through the distinction between plays and impressions. The models apply the relevant multiplier when the unit type is PLAY and retain separate quantities for offered, sold and unsold activity.

The SQL includes bid requests, bid responses, bid rate, loss reasons, supply CPM, demand CPM and spend. Sales and no-sales processing are joined back to supply so that a reporting row can represent both delivered and undelivered demand.

Null handling in the reporting layer makes absent sales measures explicit. Diagnostic queries and revised models support checking how those values affect the final calculations.

Pacing: comparing expected and delivered activity

Developed the analytical relationship between allocation data and actual trading data. Allocation processing expands per-date/per-hour structures and matches the share-of-time and impression entries by their hour key.

The model selects the latest allocation for the relevant market/deal and combines it with the appropriate deal state. It enriches the result with asset information, including time-zone context.

Expected activity is then compared with trading records at campaign, placement and hour level. Calculations include expected and sold impressions, unsold impressions, discrepancies, actual revenue and expected revenue.

Time zones and campaign-level context

Converted locally defined allocation times to UTC before matching them to trading hours. Retained local and UTC timestamps so that the report could explain the business period as well as the technical join.

Used window calculations for campaign totals and start/end boundaries. The reporting SQL derives total allocated impressions, target revenue and whether the campaign period is current, future or past.

The architecture review also identified cases where window functions could replace repeated aggregate-and-join constructions, making the transformation easier to read and maintain.

Late-arriving data and historical repair

Analysed the effect of Kafka lag on hourly data completeness. A file-loading run can finish while records for that hour are still arriving in S3, so loading success does not by itself establish that an hour is complete.

Prepared SQL for missing-hour investigation, loading selected hours and backfilling date ranges. The working set includes separate UAT and production-oriented scripts and parameterised time windows.

These tasks connected operational observations with data repair: identify the affected period, inspect the source and target records, rerun the relevant processing and compare the result. The historical scripts document the development and troubleshooting work rather than a current operational SLA.

Verification and analysis tools

Used SQL and Jupyter analysis to compare pacing, allocation, supply and demand results. Worked with report exports and sample cases to investigate differences in the figures seen by users.

DataDog and Grafana supported operational investigation alongside the data-level checks. QuickSight and ThoughtSpot were part of the reporting environment, with Athena and Lambda in the wider AWS stack.

Real-time architecture review and recommendations

Reviewed an allocation-report requirement in which users needed to inspect inventory distribution before saving a deal. The requested views included pivot/drill-down analysis by time and frame, impressions, share of time and price, with local-time presentation and export.

Prepared recommendations around Kafka stream/table joins, typed Avro records, precomputed aggregates and an operational serving database such as PostgreSQL with a JavaScript visualisation layer. These were architecture proposals for reducing the work performed at report-request time.

The proposal included phased implementation and performance testing. It is separate from the delivered Snowflake/dbt model development and is not described as an already deployed replacement platform.

Engineering contribution and handover

The work joined analytical modelling with operational troubleshooting: understanding nested advertising events, preserving the intended grain, reconciling allocations and sales, repairing incomplete periods and documenting alternatives for lower-latency use cases.

Delivered SQL/dbt changes, analysis notebooks, backfill and checking scripts, and architecture-review material that explained both the data logic and the processing constraints.
