Six connected functional areas

The EU-funded platform covered transport monitoring; training and examinations for drivers and safety advisers; electronic permits; integrations; user/role administration; and reporting and dashboards.

The architectural challenge was to make those areas share consistent business entities, permissions and evidence, while accommodating external systems and dependencies.

Solution architecture and shared domain model

Defined architectural views, service/module boundaries and data responsibilities. Aligned organisations, people, vehicles, transport plans, permits and supervisory results across the system.

Specified access controls, audit trails and traceability through multiple modules. Developed requirements for DEV, TEST and DEMO/UAT, together with production preparation, monitoring, logging, backup/recovery and operational ownership.

Business analysis translated into delivery specifications

Prepared business-process descriptions, use cases, business rules, state models, screen catalogues, data models, SQL inputs, API requirements, RBAC rules and test scenarios.

Maintained traceability matrices so requirements, design decisions, implementation tasks and acceptance evidence could be followed together. Updated specifications after workshops, interface clarification and UAT findings.

API and external integration design

Defined integration boundaries for identity, vehicle data, electronic signatures, tachograph/transport systems and freight-information exchange. The work included NIAS/e-Poslovanje, CVH, SOTAH/AFFINIS, eFTI/NSCP and electronic-signature service interfaces.

Prepared service catalogues, field mappings and mock/test approaches while external interface details were being confirmed. Specified API contracts, versioning, validation, idempotency, retries, error handling, security and audit behaviour.

Tracked unresolved dependencies such as endpoint/protocol confirmation, access rights and data freshness. Interface design and validation are distinguished from the activation of every external integration in production.

UAT, access testing and acceptance evidence

Prepared test plans, checklists and acceptance criteria across all six modules and delivery stages. Covered functional behaviour, authentication, authorisation, integration and end-to-end business processes.

Verified user, organisation and role contexts; included positive and negative permission tests and cross-module continuation. Re-read results in the subsequent business step to check that a change had the intended visible effect.

Recorded expected/actual behaviour, PASS/PARTIAL/FAIL/BLOCKED outcomes, evidence, test-data identifiers, blockers and retest conditions. Escalated access/security defects and broken process flows with actionable correction requirements.

Technical leadership and stakeholder coordination

Organised the backlog, priorities, delivery sequence, risks, dependencies and open decisions. Coordinated engineering, infrastructure, security, integrations and validation.

Worked with the agency, ministry and other public/technical stakeholders to translate transport and freight-information requirements into implementation decisions. Maintained decision records and ownership of unresolved questions.

Prepared status and readiness reports, workshops and inputs to Go/No-Go decisions. The reference covers architecture, coordination and validation; final acceptance and production activation remain separate delivery milestones.
